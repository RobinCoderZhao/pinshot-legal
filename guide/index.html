THE PINSHOT FIELD GUIDE

Your first trip with PinShot

A little setup before you leave makes it easier to give your photos the right places when you return.

IN THIS GUIDEBefore you leaveWhile you’re outImport a GPX · v1.3+When you get homeReview your resultsSupported formatsIf something doesn’t match

01 · Before you leave

Install PinShot on your iPhone and check your camera’s clock. Photo locations are matched by capture time. An incorrect date, time zone or clock setting can prevent a correct match.

  • Allow the location permissions needed for your chosen recording mode. Check the app’s permission guidance before relying on background recording.
  • Set your camera’s date and time accurately, including when traveling across time zones.
  • Try a short recording and a few supported photos before an important trip.
A matching recording is essential. Installing PinShot after a trip does not recreate its GPS history.

02 · While you’re out

Open the recording screen and start GPS recording before you take photos. Keep your iPhone with you, and check that recording has actually started.

PinShot supports background recording and adapts sampling while stationary. GPS quality and continuity still depend on location permissions, signal conditions and system behavior. Tunnels, buildings or interrupted recording may leave gaps.

When you finish, review your route and stop recording when you no longer need it.

Use the track you already have

GPX import, the interactive getting-started example and camera calibration assistant require PinShot 1.3 or later. Availability depends on the version currently released in your App Store.

A GPX file from another recorder can help when you already have photos and their corresponding recorded route. It must contain track points with real recording timestamps. A planned route without times cannot recover where an old photo was taken.

  1. Export a GPX 1.0 or 1.1 recording from your logger. Keep a copy of the original.
  2. Open Settings or Tag → Import GPX track in PinShot. Select the file and review the point count, skipped points, segments and time range.
  3. Confirm import to add a separate source. Existing recordings are kept. Reimporting the identical file reuses its source.
  4. Use the time-match preview, or select your own supported photos in Tag. Review the camera offset if the capture time does not align.

Imported tracks are matched only inside their recorded segments. Missing or invalid points break coverage, as do gaps longer than five minutes. Conflicting or overlapping tracks are rejected; export them separately. The current limit is 20 MB, 200,000 points and 10,000 segments.

Try a small fictional example

The sample pack contains a clearly labeled generated test image and synthetic GPX coordinates. It is not a real travel photo or recorded journey. Follow the included instructions, and use it only with the sample photo. Check your camera offset before testing and restore it afterward.

Calibrate a camera that runs fast or slow

Photograph a reliable clock. In Settings → Calibrate camera time, enter the camera's recorded capture time and the actual time visible in that same photo, including seconds. A positive offset means the camera is fast. The assistant changes the matching offset, not the photo's date or metadata.

03 · When you get home

Choose the photos or videos you want to geotag in the tagging screen. You can scan your photo library or select items manually. Pro adds full and custom-date scans, as well as Mac batch file and folder tagging.

  1. Make sure the GPS logs cover the media’s actual capture period.
  2. Choose the appropriate log source, especially when working with logs transferred from another device.
  3. If the camera clock was offset, review the camera time offset in Settings before matching.
  4. Start with a small selection, run tagging, and inspect the results.

Working on Mac

Bring your iPhone logs to Mac using PinShot’s own log format, or enable personal iCloud log sync with Pro. For folder workflows, keep a backup of important files before processing them.

GPX export is a Pro feature. GPX import and time-match preview are available in version 1.3 and later without Pro. Check the version available in your App Store.

04 · Review your results

Check the result summary and inspect several locations in your photo library. Review failed, skipped or unmatched items separately.

On iOS, keep the originals until you have checked the GPS-tagged copies. New copies are organized in the PinShot album; eligible originals may appear in the cleanup album for your manual review. Copies do not automatically inherit the originals’ albums. If album organization is pending, retain the originals and check again.

For edited media, the visible edited result is preserved, but earlier adjustment instructions and Revert history do not transfer. Mac Photos uses validated replacement, while Mac folder tagging may modify files in place.

05 · Supported formats

MediaSupported workflow
PhotosJPEG/JPG, HEIC/HEIF, PNG, TIFF/TIF and DNG
Live PhotosSupported Live Photos in the system photo library
VideosMP4, MOV and M4V containers, including common H.264 and HEVC/H.265 video
Camera RAWCR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF and RW2 are not modified directly. Consider a supported DNG workflow while keeping the original files.

File extensions alone do not guarantee compatibility. Unsupported or unrecognized media is skipped, and an accurate capture time is needed for matching.

06 · If something doesn’t match

No matching GPS record

Check whether recording was active at the actual capture time, whether the correct device’s logs are selected, and whether there was a recording or signal gap. No matching track means the location cannot be recovered automatically.

The time looks wrong

Check the original capture time, camera clock, time zone and configured offset. The date a file was imported into your library is not necessarily the time it was taken. Review the media details before trying another small batch.

A file is skipped

Check the supported formats above and the result shown in the app. Do not treat a skipped file as a successful geotag. For help, send the developer the file format, device, app version and error message; avoid sending private photos or exact routes unless necessary.

Using free and Pro

The free version includes up to 20 media geotags per month. Pro unlocks unlimited tagging, full/custom-date scans, export, personal iCloud log sync and Mac folder tagging. A separate lifetime purchase is available alongside monthly and yearly subscriptions. Prices and eligible offers are shown in the App Store.
